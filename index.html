AltRA-Test An Alternative Annotator Test for LLM Judges Without Shared Annotators

Nagham Omar · Rotem Dror · Almog Gueta · Avigdor Gal · Roi Reichart · Nitay Calderon

Paper Code & Data

Abstract

As LLM judges increasingly replace human annotators, a reliable statistical test is needed to decide whether an LLM judge can actually replace humans. The Alternative Annotator Test (Alt-Test; Calderon et al., 2025) was a first step, designed for dense settings in which the same annotators label every item, whereas in many large-scale settings different items have different annotators. AltRA-Test, the Alternative Annotator Test with Resampled Annotations, works in all annotation settings without requiring shared annotators, and needs only three human annotations per item, often for about 100 items. It replaces one human annotation per item with the LLM annotation and tests whether inter-annotator agreement stays as high as among the humans alone, that is, whether the LLM preserves the signal the annotators share. In simulations, where the better annotator is known, AltRA-Test makes more correct replacement decisions than existing baselines. On real data, covering 18 LLMs, 5 prompting techniques and 20 datasets across text, image, audio and video, LLMs pass in about half of the setups, depending mainly on the task.
